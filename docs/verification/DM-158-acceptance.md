## Acceptance criteria

- **c1 — drilling art:** The atlas has six distinct drill frames. The production action page displayed seven actions and changing drill frames. [Top drill](DM-158-top-drill-production.png) and [bottom drill](DM-158-bottom-drill-production.png) show the contact/spark frame on the top ticket facing left and on the lower ticket facing right, respectively. Foot anchors in the metadata place the visible feet at each card's top; the sprite stays visually contained and clean in both views.
- **c2 — stacked-board behavior:** The live production graph has four linked ticket cards. Its mascot reached and drilled on the highest running card, with feet at its top; after the preview's **Complete upper task** action changed the upper card to `ACCEPTED`, it drilled on the lower running card. The captured top fall crossed DM-2 without landing because the mascot center was outside the card's 26 px body inset; DM-3 was the first platform directly below that could support the 52 px body. Three consecutive samples put its planted foot at DM-3's top within 0.001 px. [The catch screenshot](DM-158-lower-catch-production.png) shows the result. A second shake from the bottom card put its foot at the graph floor within 0.001 px, shown in [floor-fall screenshot](DM-158-floor-fall-production.png). The accepted [DM-161 live integration record](DM-161-ui.md) additionally observed intermediate ascent, a fixed grip-rest pose followed by resumed climbing, a spontaneous slip without shake-ID change, and successful ascent to the top. Those stochastic observations were not repeated in this deterministic acceptance harness; motion unit tests cover no-active-ticket exploration, collision, and recovery paths.
- **c3 — real controls and lifecycle:** Production Edge Zoom In changed viewport scale from `0.852143` to `1.02257`; the actual Fit View button changed it to `0.836395` and settled without shake. Gentle canvas pan changed position without shake. Ticket `tk_3` selection and drag/drop callback worked. Pausing a live drill froze the entire sprite style, including spark frame, across 550 ms; reduced motion showed static idle frame 0 and `data-animated=false`. Narrow 760 px layout had 760 px scroll width and no horizontal overflow. [Paused drill](DM-158-paused-drill-production.png) and [narrow layout](DM-158-narrow-production.png) are saved. Hidden-page animation cleanup is covered by the accepted `GraphMascot` lifecycle tests rather than a browser tab-visibility simulation in this pass.
- **c4 — Fireguard:** Reused the exact accepted DM-162 report described above. Post-grade changes remove an unused type export and trailing blank lines in `shake.test.ts`; neither alters runtime or test behavior.
- **c5 — gesture separation:** A real three-leg, 70 px canvas wiggle incremented shake ID exactly `0→1` and started a tumble; a second wiggle from the bottom incremented `1→2` and fell to the floor. Gentle pan, Zoom In, and Fit View left shake ID at 0. [Top tumble](DM-158-top-tumble-production.png) and the sampled trajectory in metadata show the first event.

The production harness closes its browser and local port 5180 when finished. It does not write application domain state; its **Complete upper task** action changes only the in-memory preview fixture.
